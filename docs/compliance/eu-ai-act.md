# EU AI Act

Not legal advice, and not a statement that Tracekit meets any article ([about these pages](README.md)).

## Scope

The AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, sets rules for AI systems placed on the market, put into service or used in
the EU. What it asks of you depends on your role and on the system. A **provider** develops an AI system, or has one
developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name; a **deployer** uses an AI system
under its authority (Art. 3(3) and 3(4)). Every article mapped below applies only to **high-risk** AI systems:
products, or safety components of products, under the Union law listed in Annex I that need a third-party conformity
assessment, and systems used in the areas listed in Annex III, with the exceptions in Art. 6(3) (Art. 6). An agent
isn't high-risk because it is an agent: that depends on its intended purpose. The Act entered into force on 1 August
2024 and applies from 2 August 2026, with exceptions. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus on AI, in force
since 27 July 2026) changed when the high-risk rules of Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3 (Articles 6 to 27) apply: with
the exception of Article 6(5), from 2 December 2027 to Annex III systems, and from 2 August 2028 to Annex I systems
(Art. 113, third paragraph, point (c), as amended). Articles 50, 72 and 73 are outside those sections; when they
apply to your system is a question for your counsel. Each row says whether the duty falls on the provider or the
deployer. A provider can build Tracekit into an agent as part of its logging; a deployer that runs the signer holds
the logs.

## Not covered by Tracekit

- **Data and data governance (Art. 10).** Training, validation and test data are out of scope.
- **Technical documentation and its keeping (Art. 11, 18), conformity assessment, the EU declaration of conformity,
  CE marking and registration (Art. 43, 47, 48, 49).**
- **Accuracy and robustness levels (Art. 15(1)–(3)).** Tracekit doesn't measure a model.
- **The minimum logs for remote biometric identification (Art. 12(3)) and the two-person check (Art. 14(5)).** Each
  approval has one approver; Tracekit has no two-person rule.
- **Transparency obligations (Art. 50).** Telling people they are interacting with an AI system, and marking
  generated content.
- **General-purpose AI model obligations (Art. 53, 55).** They fall on model providers.
- **Prohibited practices (Art. 5), AI literacy (Art. 4) and the fundamental rights impact assessment (Art. 27).**
