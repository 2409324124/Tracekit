# NIST AI RMF and the Generative AI Profile

Not legal advice, and not a statement that Tracekit meets any outcome ([about these pages](README.md)).

## Scope

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1, released 26 January 2023) is a voluntary framework
from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology for managing the risks of AI systems. It has four functions,
GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE, each split into categories and subcategories. Subcategories are outcomes an
organisation reaches with people, process and technology together, not controls a product meets. The Generative AI
Profile (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024) applies the framework to generative AI: it lists 12 risks and suggests actions, each
with an ID tied to a subcategory (`MG-4.3-002` is the second suggested action for MANAGE 4.3). Neither binds anyone who
hasn't adopted it, through a contract, a policy or a regulator's expectation. NIST says AI RMF 1.0 is being revised;
this page maps 1.0. Each row below names the part Tracekit can contribute.

## Mapping: Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1)

Five of the profile's 12 risks concern what Tracekit records or controls.

| Requirement | What Tracekit provides | Evidence to show | Your responsibility / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Information Security** (§2.9). Generative AI lowers the barrier to offensive cyber operations, and widens the attack surface, for example to prompt injection and data poisoning. | Gating of the tool calls an injected or misaligned agent makes: the packs deny or hold risky calls ([packs](../policy-v2.md#packs-shipped)), and provenance rules (experimental) hold calls whose target only untrusted content brought in. Tamper-evident records. | Denied and held calls by rule id; the verify report. | Model and application security. Rules are tripwires; Tracekit doesn't reduce what a model can help an attacker do. |
| **Human-AI Configuration** (§2.7). Arrangements between people and AI can lead to anthropomorphising, algorithmic aversion, automation bias, over-reliance or emotional entanglement. | Held calls go to named people, who see the signer's copy of the arguments, not what the agent shows; their answers are recorded ([approvals](../approvals.md#flow)). | Approval records; `approvals: self` and `break-glass approvals` lines. | Automation bias in approvers: a person can approve whatever is put in front of them, and an agent can persuade them ([guarantees](../guarantees.md#outside-every-deployment)). |
| **Value Chain and Component Integration** (§2.12). Upstream third-party components can be integrated without transparency or traceability. | Records of calls to third-party tools, MCP servers and provider-run tools, as for GOVERN 6.1 above (experimental services). | T2 records with `via`; `executed_by: provider` tool uses. | Supplier vetting and the provenance of models and data. |
| **Data Privacy** (§2.4). Personal or sensitive data can leak, or be used, disclosed or de-anonymised without authorisation. | Records hold salted commitments to arguments and results, not content. The signer redacts known secret patterns before it commits to a result. Policy rules can deny secrets in arguments and, with provenance (experimental), hold mail to recipients that only untrusted content brought in ([privacy](../privacy.md#the-v2-signer)). | `redaction` manifests on `tool.result`; denied calls (`TK-S002`, `TK-S003`). | Data protection as a whole. Redaction is pattern-based and misses secrets it doesn't recognise; commitments hide content, not timing, sizes or counts ([limits](../limits.md#privacy)). |
| **Information Integrity** (§2.8). Content that doesn't distinguish fact from opinion or fiction, and large-scale mis- and disinformation. | Integrity of the record of the agent's actions only: who did what, under which policy, unchanged since it was signed. | Bundles and verify reports. | The integrity of generated content. Tracekit doesn't check outputs for truth. |

Suggested actions where Tracekit's records are an input (each is an action for your organisation, not for a tool):

| Requirement | What Tracekit provides | Evidence to show | Your responsibility / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MG-4.3-002**. Record and track reported errors, near-misses and negative impacts. | Denied and held calls, signed gaps and incidents are near-misses already on the record. | Decisions, gaps, incident records. | Errors and impacts outside agent actions; the tracking process. |
| **MS-4.2-004**. Monitor and document instances where people or other systems override the system's decisions. | Every rejection of a held call, and every decision that denied a call the agent asked for, is a signed record. | `approval` records with decision `reject`; `policy.decision` with `deny`. | Evaluating why overrides happen. |
| **GV-4.3-002**. Identify the minimum criteria for incident reporting (system id, title, reporter, date, description, impacts, ...). | An incident record carries its id, trigger, rule ids, scope, the records it cites and the time; `incident.resolved` adds who resolved it and why. | `incident.*` records. | Defining the criteria, and the fields Tracekit doesn't hold (impact, affected stakeholders). |
| **MG-4.3-001**. After-action assessments of incidents check that response and recovery processes were followed and effective. | Bundles of the runs involved, with the verifier's check that each containment response was honoured (`incidents` line, [incidents](../incidents.md#what-the-verifier-checks)). | Bundles; verify reports. | The assessment. |
| **GV-6.2-002**. Document incidents involving third-party GAI data and systems. | Incidents and records of calls through the MCP gateway and executor name the upstream tool (experimental). | Incident records; T2 records. | Documenting the third party's role and contacting it. |

## Not covered by Tracekit

- **MAP 1 and MAP 3.1–3.4: context, intended purpose, benefits and costs.** Tracekit doesn't establish context.
- **MEASURE 2.1–2.3, 2.5, 2.6, 2.9–2.13: test sets, validity, reliability, safety evaluation, explainability, privacy
  risk evaluation, fairness and bias, and environmental impact.** Tracekit doesn't evaluate models.
- **GOVERN 2 (training and accountability structures), GOVERN 5 (external feedback) and MEASURE 3.3 (feedback from
  end users).**
- **AI 600-1 risks it doesn't address:** CBRN Information or Capabilities; Confabulation; Dangerous, Violent, or Hateful
  Content; Environmental Impacts; Harmful Bias and Homogenization; Intellectual Property; Obscene, Degrading, and/or
  Abusive Content.
- **Content provenance for generated content** (AI 600-1's primary consideration of that name): Tracekit records
  where an argument's values came from within a run, not where generated media came from.
